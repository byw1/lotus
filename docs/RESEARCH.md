# Sourcing

This is the document that keeps the site honest.

Everything this website states about the Los Angeles Lotus Festival came from
somewhere, and this file records where. More usefully, it records the places
where the festival's **own sources contradict each other** — and what the site
therefore says instead. Those are the passages that look oddly careful when you
read them. They are careful on purpose, and this is the file that explains why.

If you are about to make a sentence on this site punchier, read this first.

---

## The rule

**A civic nonprofit's website is not the place to settle a disputed history by
picking the better story.** Where accounts conflict, the site either reports that
accounts differ, or says something narrower that is true under all of them.
Where nothing has been confirmed, the site says "to be announced" and leaves a
`TODO(confirm)` in `src/config/site.ts`.

Nothing on this site is a guess presented as a fact.

## Which sources outrank which

1. **The City of Los Angeles Department of Recreation and Parks**, on its own
   pages and in its own PDFs — the festival's producer since 1972.
2. **The festival's official program booklet and sponsor packet**, published by
   the same department.
3. **The City's application PDFs** on `laparks.org` and
   `recreation.parks.lacity.gov` — the paper forms vendors, performers, food
   booths, volunteers and dragon boat teams actually fill in. These are the best
   source for how the festival works operationally, because they are the
   festival telling applicants what it requires.
4. **`lacity.gov` news releases**, for the honored country in a given year.
5. **Local press** — the Eastsider, Echo Park Now, the Beverly Press, ABC7 —
   for corroboration and for things the City does not publish.
6. **Blogs and third-party listings**, for leads only. Never as the sole basis
   for a claim on this site.

Two cautions about the sources themselves:

- **The 45th program booklet's PDF is a decoding problem.** Its subset font has a
  scrambled encoding: the digits 8 and 9 render as nothing, and several letters
  map to control characters. The substitution map was reverse-engineered to read
  it, but **any digit that was an 8 or a 9 is unrecoverable**. That is why the
  phone number is missing from this site, below.
- **The festival's own site, `lotusfestivalla.com`, is a partly-unconfigured
  template.** As of September 2026 every page carries a placeholder contact block
  reading `555-555-5555` and `mymail@mailservice.com`, several images are
  captioned "Photo By: John Doe", and the vendor link still points at the 44th
  festival's application. It is a good source for the festival's own account of
  its history and its list of past honored countries; it is not a source for
  contact details or current-year logistics.

## Where the sources contradict each other

### 1. The dragon boat origin — three incompatible accounts

There is no agreed story for how dragon boat racing came to this festival, and
all three versions come from festival-affiliated sources:

- **1975, the third festival.** The City's official wording, repeated verbatim in
  the 2020 dragon boat application and the 2026 program booklet: the races "have
  been a part of the Lotus Festival tradition since their introduction in 1975
  during the 3rd Lotus Festival." The boats were reportedly built from two
  rowboats lashed together and fitted with dragon décor; ten co-ed teams raced.
- **1991, a Chinese opening ceremony.** [RAP's own live
  page](https://recreation.parks.lacity.gov/lotusfestival) says the races "became
  an iconic part of the festival after a Chinese opening ceremony in early 1991."
- **The mid-1990s, via a board chair.** The festival's own site credits the Hon.
  Bob Gin, Advisory Board chair 1994–1995, as "instrumental in introducing the
  Dragon Boat Race to the festival" —
  [lotusfestivalla.com/about-lotus](http://www.lotusfestivalla.com/about-lotus).

The 1975 date is also arithmetically odd: if 1972 was the first festival in an
annual series, the third would have been 1974, and the one blog that ties the
third festival to China dates it to 1974 rather than 1975.

**What the site says:** the history entry for 1975 gives the City's account and
then states plainly that the festival's own records tell this story more than one
way, naming all three. Elsewhere the site says "for fifty years" rather than
picking a year. It never asserts one version as fact.

### 2. The size of the lotus bed — two conflicting official claims

- **"Largest lotus bed in the United States"** — current City and press
  materials, including the [2025 sponsor
  booklet](https://recreation.parks.lacity.gov/sites/default/files/images/lotus/2025/Sponsor%20Booklet%202025.pdf),
  the [Crescenta Valley
  Weekly](https://www.crescentavalleyweekly.com/between-friends/07/17/2025/the-44th-lotus-festival-attracts-thousands-to-echo-park-lake/),
  and Rep. Judy Chu's 2026 congratulatory letter reproduced in the program
  booklet (p.12).
- **"Largest in the Western U.S."** — the Echo Park Historical Society, via
  [Echo Park Now](https://echoparknow.com/2010/07/10/brief-history-of-the-lotus-festival/),
  noting larger beds in the Carolinas, Hawaii and possibly Florida.

**What the site says:** neither. `site.venue.note` reads "one of the largest
lotus beds in the country, planted about a century ago and in bloom every July"
and describes the bed rather than ranking it. **Do not write "largest in the
United States"** on this site, however many City documents say it — the City's
own historical partner says otherwise, and the claim is not ours to adjudicate.

### 3. Attendance — three different numbers

- **"More than 125,000 attendees each year"** — 45th program booklet p.2; the
  2025 sponsor booklet says "125,000+".
- **"Over 120,000 attendees over the course of two days"** — RAP spokesperson
  Rose Watson, quoted by [the Eastsider in
  2022](https://www.theeastsiderla.com/neighborhoods/echo_park/the-lotus-festival-returns---live---to-echo-park-lake-in-july/article_e06ef716-e7ae-11ec-8b65-4f8d76a55e9d.html).
- **"Over 100,000"** — elsewhere in the *same* sponsor packet PDF, on the same
  page as the 125,000 figure.

A document that disagrees with itself is not a document you can quote precisely.

**What the site says:** always "more than 125,000 people each year" — the
current official figure, always as a floor, **never as a precise count**. There
is a comment on `site.attendance` saying exactly this.

### 4. The 1978–1980 pause

The City's own program booklet (p.2) says the festival paused **from 1978 to
1980** because of City budget cuts, returning as "The Lotus Festival". A personal
blog states the pause ran "between 1978 and 1990" — a twelve-year gap rather than
a three-year one.

**What the site says:** 1978–1980, following the City. The blog is a single
secondary source contradicting the producer's own published history, and it is
not enough to move the claim. The site's history entry for 1978 says only "City
budget cuts halt the festival for two years."

A related claim in early planning notes — a "1980–85 sponsor-driven revival" —
has **no source anywhere** and appears nowhere on this site.

### 5. The 2011 cancellation that did not happen

[RAP's page](https://recreation.parks.lacity.gov/lotusfestival) states that "in
2011, the Lotus Festival was canceled to allow for restoration efforts." Local
reporting from [July
2011](http://www.echoparknow.com/2011/07/01/the-33rd-lotus-festival-returns-to-echo-park-lake-next-weekend/)
covers the 33rd festival taking place on 9–10 July that year; the lake
rehabilitation began later that summer, and the festival was absent in 2012 and
2013.

**What the site says:** the 2012 history entry describes the bed dying off by
2010, the lake closing for rehabilitation and the festival pausing for two years.
It does not name 2011 as a cancelled year.

### 6. Why the site never says "46 years"

Forty-five editions have run across the fifty-five calendar years from 1972 to
2026. The documented gaps — 1978, 1979, 1980, 2009, 2012, 2013 and 2020, with
2021 virtual only — account for some of that difference but **not all of it**,
and the pre-2010 edition numbers cannot be reconstructed reliably from any
available source.

**What the site says:** "the 46th annual Los Angeles Lotus Festival, a tradition
since 1972." That construction is true. "46 years", "46 consecutive years" and
"celebrating 46 years" are all false, and a continuous 1972→2027 table of honored
countries cannot be published, because one does not exist.

### 7. Ellen Quan

Early notes for this project referred to "Ella Quan". The City's own program
booklet honor roll (p.9) reads **ELLEN QUAN**, 1972–1977, alongside Helen Young.

**What the site says:** Ellen. Getting a founder's name right is the least a
festival's website owes her.

### 8. Why there is no phone number on this site

The festival's phone number appears in the 45th program booklet (p.20) as
`213-4_5-1310` — the second digit of the exchange is an 8 or a 9, destroyed by
the PDF's font encoding. The most likely reconstruction is (213) 485-1310, since
RAP uses the 213-485 exchange, but that is an inference. Yelp separately lists
(213) 485-5027, unconfirmed and third-party. The festival's own website shows the
placeholder `555-555-5555`.

**What the site says:** nothing. `site.contact.phone` is `null`, with a
`TODO(confirm)` explaining why, and every page routes people to
`lotus.festival@lacity.org` instead. **A wrong phone number on a civic site sends
people nowhere for years.** Do not add one until someone at Recreation and Parks
confirms it.

### 9. The 2027 honoring of China had not been announced when this was built

The honored country is announced at the previous festival's **closing ceremony**,
on the Sunday evening. As of September 2026, **no public announcement of the 46th
festival's honored country had appeared** in press coverage or on the City's
page. What does exist is a "46th Lotus Festival Interest Survey" Google form
linked from the festival's site, gathering vendor interest for "next year's 2027
event" — evidence that planning is under way, and silent on the country.

**What the site says:** China, as **the festival's own statement of what is
coming** — never as reported news, never with a citation it does not have.
`site.honoredCountry.announced` is `false` and carries a `TODO(confirm)` saying
so. If this site goes live before the announcement is made, that flag is the
thing to check.

Note also that the 38th festival (2018) appears to have honored China, but one
search-index snippet said "Republic of China" (Taiwan) and another "People's
Republic of China". **Do not state which China 2018 honored** without
re-verifying it, and do not build a "the last time we honored China" line on it.

## Checked, and not used

Three claims were investigated and left off the site because they did not hold
up:

1. **"The Los Angeles Racing Dragons run the dragon boat races."** They do not.
   [LARD](https://laracingdragons.org/history) is an unrelated Long Beach club,
   founded 1996, whose published race schedule does not include the Lotus
   Festival and whose site never mentions it. The races are run by Los Angeles
   Lotus Festival, Inc. with Recreation and Parks.
2. **"The third festival honored China."** The dragon-boat half of that sentence
   is officially stated; the China half rests on a single blog and is
   uncorroborated by any City source.
3. **A certificate-of-insurance requirement for vendors.** Not present in any of
   the City's application PDFs. It may well be real; it is not documented, so the
   site does not state it.

Officer names and financial figures from the nonprofit's Form 990-EZ are also
absent: ProPublica's PDF endpoint returned a bot challenge, so nothing beyond the
IRS Business Master File record (EIN, subsection, ruling date, deductibility) was
independently verified.

## Adding a fact to this site

1. Find a source. Prefer the City, then the festival's own printed materials,
   then the application PDFs, then `lacity.gov`, then local press.
2. Look for a second source, especially for anything the site will state as a
   number, a date or a superlative.
3. If two sources disagree, **do not choose**. Either write the narrower sentence
   that is true under both, or say that accounts differ — and leave a comment in
   `src/config/` recording the conflict, so the next person does not spend an
   afternoon rediscovering it.
4. If you cannot source it, leave a `TODO(confirm)` and let the UI say "to be
   announced".
5. Add a row to the table above, with the URL.

Every claim on this site should be traceable from this file. If you find one that
is not, that is a bug worth an issue.
